# Training-time audit of boundary-level supervision conflicts

We performed a separately identified observational training run per encoder using the locked matched-70 corpus, fold 1, seed 42, pinned pretrained initialization and the existing 12-epoch development protocol. The preserved SecureBERT run completed on October 28 (Linux 6.18.55, glibc 2.39); the reported RoBERTa run was initialized anew on November 6 (Linux 6.18.57, glibc 2.41), because the saved November 1 epoch-6 recovery requires glibc 2.39. Earlier RoBERTa attempts, including a locally completed November 5 run without a complete saved checkpoint, were lost during workspace maintenance and are excluded. No recovery identity was rewritten. Cross-environment numerical parity is not established. Diagnostic logging retained the original candidate construction, sampler, objective, optimizer and validation selection. Each epoch traversed 49 training windows from 36 documents; no test evaluation or gold-label correction was performed.

A conflict group comprises the same directed endpoint boundaries with both positive and negative existence targets in one sampler call. Eligible-pool conflicts are reported separately from those actually sampled. Counts across windows and epochs are repeated observations, not independent relations.

The measured distinction between eligible conflicts and realized sampled exposure motivates a controlled boundary-consistency ablation before combining any correction with hard-negative mining. These counts do not establish that conflicts caused prior top-2 decoding degradation or encoder differences. Frequency does not directly quantify gradient influence. Validation was reused for selection, and no generalization or significance claim is made. On November 8, the complete RoBERTa recovery was reconstructed from three hash-verified compressed parts. Its missing prediction export was regenerated on the same five validation documents using the frozen selected checkpoint and threshold; metrics exactly matched the preserved metrics. SecureBERT predictions were restored and rescored. This was a validation-only forward, not retraining; identity-level parity with the missing RoBERTa prediction file cannot be established. Complete candidate pools and intermediate step states were not exported; auditing checks recorded counts, schedules, serialized predictions and checkpoint consistency rather than reconstructing every neural computation.
